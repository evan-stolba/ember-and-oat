Our Story

A coffee shop with no walls — and no plug.

How one retrofitted delivery van became a rolling experiment in sustainable, community-first coffee.

Why we started

Most coffee shops run on grid electricity, single-use cups, and expensive downtown leases. We wanted to see if great coffee could be made without any of them. In 2026 we bought a retired delivery van, covered its roof with 2.4 kW of solar panels, and installed a lithium battery bank big enough to run a commercial espresso machine for a full eight-hour day.

The result is Ember & Oat: a coffee truck that goes to where people already are — campuses, farmers' markets, trailheads and office parks — instead of asking them to come to us.

How it works

1

Charge

Panels charge the batteries while we drive and while we serve. A sunny morning tops us up by noon.

2

Brew

We pull every shot from single-origin beans roasted in small batches by a local partner roaster.

3

Serve

Drinks go out in compostable cups — or your own mug, which earns you 50¢ off.

4

Return

Grounds are collected and donated to community gardens as compost each week.

Our business model

Ember & Oat earns revenue through three channels:

  • Daily street service at a rotating schedule of high-foot-traffic locations, posted each week on our site and social media.
  • Private event catering for weddings, corporate offsites and campus events, where a quiet, generator-free truck is a real selling point.
  • The Sun Club subscription — $39/month for one drink a day, which gives us predictable revenue and gives regulars a deal.

Because we have no rent and almost no energy bill, our fixed costs are roughly 60% lower than a typical café, which lets us pay baristas above market wage while keeping drink prices competitive.

What's next

Our goal is a small fleet of three trucks by 2028, plus a mobile app for pre-ordering so customers can skip the line. Have a question or want to book us for an event? Ask our chatbot in the corner — it's open 24/7, even when the sun isn't.
